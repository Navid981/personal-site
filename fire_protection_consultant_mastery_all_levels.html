1. Fire Protection Fundamentals

Detection
Detect fire early: smoke, heat, flame, etc.
Suppression
Control/extinguish fire with water, clean agent, foam, etc.
Containment
Rated barriers, doors and compartmentation slow spread.
Life safety
Protect occupants through egress, alarms and smoke control.

3. NFPA 13 — Sprinkler Design

Step-by-step

  1. Identify occupancy/storage hazard.
  2. Select applicable sprinkler type.
  3. Determine design criteria.
  4. Check ceiling and obstruction conditions.
  5. Lay out sprinklers.
  6. Select remote/design area.
  7. Size branch lines/mains.
  8. Hydraulically calculate.
  9. Compare water supply.
  10. Coordinate with HVAC, lighting, structure and ceiling.

High-risk consultant doubts

“4 ft is always the minimum between sprinklers.” Wrong approach: spacing depends on the applicable hazard, sprinkler type and conditions.
“900 mm from diffuser is always acceptable.” Wrong approach: evaluate the actual obstruction/discharge condition and governing requirement.
Right mindset: identify the exact condition first, then verify the exact applicable paragraph/table/listing.

4. Hydraulic Calculation

Workflow

  1. Hazard/design criteria
  2. Remote area
  3. Sprinkler discharge
  4. Pipe friction
  5. Fittings/losses
  6. Elevation
  7. Hose allowance where required
  8. Total demand
  9. Water supply comparison
Q = K√P

Review checklist

Correct remote area? Correct density/design method? Correct K-factor? Pipe diameter? Material/coefficient? Elevation? Fittings? Hose allowance? Water-supply data? Required pressure at inlet?

Common mistake: a beautiful hydraulic sheet with the wrong remote area is still a wrong design.

2D Diagram — Fire Pump Arrangement

5. NFPA 20 — Fire Pump

The pump supplies the flow and pressure needed when the available water supply cannot satisfy the fire-system demand.

Master

Rated flow • rated pressure • pump curve • churn • rated-point performance • driver • controller • suction • test arrangement • jockey pump • room requirements.

Correct: compare system demand with the pump performance at the required duty point.
Wrong: “The pump is bigger, therefore it is automatically better.” Flow alone does not prove suitability.

6. NFPA 14 — Standpipe

Study Class I/II/III concepts, hose connections, risers, required flow/pressure, FDC, pressure-regulating devices, hydraulic calculation and high-rise pressure zoning.

15. Design Review

  1. What code/edition governs?
  2. Is hazard/occupancy correct?
  3. Are geometry and room data correct?
  4. Are device/pipe locations correct?
  5. Does the hydraulic/electrical calculation demonstrate it?
  6. Does equipment meet duty?
  7. Does it physically fit?
  8. Can it be inspected/tested/maintained?
  9. Does it coordinate with HVAC/electrical/architecture?
  10. Do drawings, calculations and specifications agree?

17. Full Consultant Workflow

  1. Collect architecture and room/occupancy data.
  2. Identify the adopted code edition and authority requirements.
  3. Write design criteria before drawing.
  4. Develop plans, risers and details.
  5. Perform/review calculations.
  6. Coordinate with architecture, structure, electrical and HVAC.
  7. Issue traceable consultant comments and close them with evidence.

18. Calculation Practice

CalculationInputsConsultant trap
Sprinkler QK and pressureWrong K or incompatible units.
Pipe frictionFlow, diameter, length, roughness, fittingsIgnoring fittings or using unjustified factors.
ElevationElevation differenceForgetting pressure head.
NACCurrent + voltage dropChecking current only.
PumpSystem demand + supply + curveSelecting by maximum flow only.

Consultant rule: every input should have a source: drawing, code criterion, manufacturer data, survey, room data or approved assumption.

19. Submittal Review

Sprinklers

Type, temperature rating, K-factor, orientation, listing/approval, spacing and obstruction compliance.

Valves

Size, rating, supervisory/tamper, flow switch, drain/test and accessibility.

Pump

Model, rated flow/head, curve, driver, controller, approvals, accessories and test arrangement.

Fire Alarm

Panel/loop/NAC capacity, compatibility, power, batteries, modules, appliances and interfaces.

Approval sequence: submittal → approved design → calculations → specifications → code/listing → coordination → comments → resubmittal verification.

20. Consultant Comment Lab

Weak: “Sprinkler is wrong. Change it.”
Strong: “Please revise the sprinkler location and submit the applicable code/design basis demonstrating compliance with the adopted spacing and obstruction requirements. Coordinate with the diffuser/ceiling layout and update calculations if the remote-area geometry changes.”
Weak: “Pump is too big.”
Strong: “Please demonstrate pump suitability against the calculated system demand and water-supply data. Submit the pump curve and identify the selected duty point.”

Strong comments are specific, traceable, actionable and technically defensible.

21. Advanced Quiz

Q1. Same pipe diameter but extra elbows: automatically acceptable?
