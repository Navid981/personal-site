What This Tool Does

Three linked modules that mirror the core pieces of a building HVAC load/energy workflow — modeled on what software like Carrier's HAP does, using standard published ASHRAE methods rather than a proprietary hourly simulation engine. Each module's output feeds the next one, so you generally work through them left to right.

Worked Example 1 — Zone Load (matches the room pre-loaded in Tab 1)

Room "Guest Room 501 (corner)": west wall 18 m² (U=1.8, absorptance 0.6), south wall 12 m² (same construction), west window 5 m² (U=2.8, SHGC=0.55, IAC=0.85), 2 occupants, 120 W lighting, 250 W equipment, 15 L/s infiltration. Site: Dhaka, design day 15 Apr, outdoor design 37°C (8°C daily range), indoor 24°C/50%RH, 10% safety factor. The engine scans all 24 hours; here is the governing hour, 15:00:

StepValue
Solar altitude / azimuth at 15:00 (from solar position equations)37.7° / 276.6°
Clear-sky beam normal irradiance856 W/m²
Irradiance on west-facing surface (incidence-angle corrected)742 W/m²
Outdoor dry-bulb at 15:00 (from daily range profile)36.8 °C
Sol-air temp, west wall = 36.8 + (0.6×742)/1762.9 °C
West wall Q = 1.8 × 18 × (62.9−24)1,262 W
South wall Q (irradiance only 68 W/m² at this hour) = 1.8 × 12 × (39.2−24)328 W
Window conduction = 2.8 × 5 × (36.8−24)179 W
Window solar gain = 5 × 0.55 × 0.85 × 7421,734 W
People sensible/latent = 2 × 70 / 2 × 45140 / 90 W
Lighting + equipment120 + 250 W
Infiltration sensible = 1.23 × 15 × (36.8−24)235 W
Infiltration latent = 3010 × 15 × Δ humidity ratio (0.0168)759 W
Sensible 4,247 W + Latent 849 W, × 1.10 safety factor5,606 W = 5.61 kW = 1.59 TR
Open Tab 1 with the default room loaded — it shows exactly 5.61 kW at 15:00. If you change any input, the number will move; the arithmetic above is what moves it.

Worked Example 2 — Energy & Cost

Using a peak block load of 6.90 kW (two rooms) at design conditions (outdoor 37°C, indoor 24°C):

StepValue
Effective building UA = peak load ÷ design ΔT = 6.90 ÷ (37−24)0.531 kW/°C
April: avg outdoor 29°C, base temp 24°C, 30 days → CDD = (29−24)×30150 °C·day
April cooling energy = UA × CDD × 24 ÷ COP = 0.531 × 150 × 24 ÷ 3.2597 kWh
Repeat for all 12 months and sum → annual kWh, then × tariff→ annual cost
This is the standard variable-base degree-day method: it assumes the building's heat gain/loss scales roughly linearly with outdoor temperature, calibrated using the one peak-load data point from Tab 1. It's a reasonable planning-level estimate — not a substitute for full 8760-hour simulation with real hourly weather (TMY) data when the stakes are financing or code-compliance numbers.

Worked Example 3 — Equipment Selection

Using the same 6.90 kW block load, diversity 0.9, 10% safety, N+1 redundancy:

StepValue
Raw block load = 6.90 kW1.96 TR
Design load = 1.96 × 0.9 diversity × 1.10 safety1.94 TR
N+1 needs each unit to independently cover the full design load — next standard step ≥ 1.94 TR2 TR per unit
Configuration: 2 × 2 TR units (1 duty + 1 standby)4 TR installed, 106% margin
N+1 doubles installed capacity because each unit must be able to carry the full load alone. Compare that to "2×50%" in Tab 3, which sizes each unit for half the load and installs less total spare capacity but has no full backup if one unit fails.

Design Conditions shared across all rooms

Picking a Location sets latitude, longitude and standard meridian (fixed geography — these never change afterward) and jumps to that division's typical peak month. Outdoor design DB, daily range and outdoor RH then update automatically for whichever Design month is selected — change the month on its own to check a different season without leaving climate values from another month behind. Winter design DB always reflects the site's coldest month. All of this stays editable if you have more precise site data. Indoor setpoint, indoor RH and supply air ΔT are your design choices and are never overwritten.
Peak load is found by scanning all 24 hours of the design day for each room (sol-air temperature + ASHRAE clear-sky solar model) and taking the governing hour — orientations peak at different times, so this is not a single fixed hour. This is a simplified peak-design method (sol-air / clear-sky, no thermal-mass time-lag), intended for preliminary and mid-design sizing rather than full 8760-hour radiant-time-series simulation.
